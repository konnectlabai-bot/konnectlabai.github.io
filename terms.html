6.1 ThresZone — Definition

ThresZone (a contraction of "Threshold Zone") is a proprietary training metric and algorithmic evaluation framework developed by Konnect International Limited for the Kyniskis platform.

The term combines two concepts:

Threshold — derived from "thresh" (to separate grain from chaff, to discern the boundary) and "hold" (to maintain position, to sustain). In training physiology, the threshold marks the boundary between productive adaptation and non-productive overload. It is the line where stimulus becomes growth — neither too little nor too much. To "hold" the threshold is the discipline of staying precisely at this edge.

Zone — the continuous operational range within which the body's neuroendocrine system responds optimally. When you train inside this zone, the hypothalamus and pituitary regulate cortisol rhythm, testosterone release, and growth hormone timing — producing a state of hormonal equilibrium that sustains efficient fat metabolism, complete recovery, and progressive strength adaptation over time.

The ThresZone metric integrates cardiovascular load, movement volume, and daily activity data, processed through a personalized baseline (derived from each user's 30-day training history) and a hyperbolic tangent (tanh) scaling model. The result is a 0-100 score that quantifies how much adaptive stimulus a user has applied on a given day — guiding them to remain inside the growth zone, at the edge of what their body can handle, without exceeding what it can recover from.

ThresZone is a trademark of Konnect International Limited. Unauthorized use of the term, the underlying algorithm, or any derivative implementations is prohibited.
